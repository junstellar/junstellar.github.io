In [Episode 6](/en/p/power-money-river/) I drew the delta where power money splits into generation, transmission, distribution and cooling. Today we go down to the **very bottom** of that river. Just as Episode 7 of the semiconductor series covered materials, parts and equipment, power has its own version — and the headline name is **copper**.

You hear it everywhere lately: "AI drove copper up." And copper did surge to **$14,527 per tonne** on the LME on January 29 this year, a record high, with the largest single-day gain since the 2008 financial crisis. It has since eased to the $13,300–13,600 range.

But digging into the numbers, I found something odd.

## Reversal one — data centers use less than 2% of copper

The world will consume roughly **28.7 million tonnes** of refined copper in 2026. Data centers' slice of that, by J.P. Morgan's estimate, is about **475,000 tonnes** — **under 2%.** Even the IEA sees data centers reaching only about 2% of demand by 2030.

How does something worth 2% of demand push a price to an all-time high? It sounds impossible — until you **change the denominator.**

![Data centers' share of copper demand — 2% of the total, a quarter of the growth](copper-illusion.png)

Copper demand in 2026 grows by roughly **450,000 tonnes** year over year (up 1.6%). Of that increment, AI data centers account for about **110,000 tonnes**. Two percent of the whole, but **a quarter of the growth.**

And there's a kicker. The International Copper Study Group (ICSG) expects the 2026 copper market to run a deficit of about **150,000 tonnes.** The 110,000 tonnes of new demand AI created is nearly the size of this year's entire shortfall.

This is how commodity markets work. **When inventories are on the floor, the price is set not by the total share but by the last tonne.** Put 100 people in a restaurant with 99 chairs, and the person who arrives last sets the price. Data centers are playing that last-arriving role.

## And supply takes 18 years

If that's demand, why can't supply catch up? A copper mine takes an average of **17.9 years from discovery to first commercial production.**

![Why copper supply lags — 17.9 years to production, falling ore grades, mine accidents](copper-supply-lag.png)

What's striking is that **the longest stretch is not "technology" but "permitting."** Exploration and discovery take 1–5 years, construction 3–5 years — but permitting and environmental review alone take 5–10 years. No amount of money shortens that stretch. In [Episode 3](/en/p/transformer-supercycle/) I called a 3–5 year transformer lead time a bottleneck; copper's is far longer.

Three things compound it.

- **Falling ore grades**: average grades have dropped below **0.6%**, half of what they were 25 years ago. Getting the same copper means digging deeper and processing far more rock.
- **Major accidents**: Grasberg in Indonesia, one of the world's largest mines, was halted by a collapse in September 2025. Lost output through end-2026 comes to **591,000 tonnes**, with full recovery pushed to 2028.
- **The long gap**: S&P Global projects world copper production **peaking at 33 million tonnes in 2030** and then declining, while demand climbs to 42 million tonnes by 2040 — a **10 million tonne shortfall.**

## That said, the experts split in opposite directions

Read only that far and it sounds like copper can only go up. But gather the institutional forecasts and even the direction disagrees.

| Institution | 2026 balance |
|---|---|
| ICSG | 150k tonne **deficit** |
| J.P. Morgan | 330k tonne **deficit** |
| Morgan Stanley | 600k tonne **deficit** |
| **Goldman Sachs** | 160k tonne **surplus** |

Goldman Sachs stands alone calling for a **surplus**, on the logic that Chinese demand is weaker than assumed and that new projects plus smelting capacity will prevent an acute physical squeeze. It expects copper to struggle to hold above $11,000 per tonne — some 20% below today's price.

The cautious case has real substance too. **Aluminum substitution** is the main one: aluminum conducts at only 61% of copper's rate but weighs a third as much and costs far less. Using it in wiring can cut copper needs by 5–15%, and as prices spiked some manufacturers did start switching. **Rising scrap supply** cushions as well — high prices flush metal out of warehouses and into the market.

My own read: copper's long-term shortage is structural (18 years for a mine is not a negotiable number), but the short-term price has already priced much of that in. The move from January's $14,527 peak down to today's $13,300s is itself the market catching its breath.

## Reversal two — cable stocks aren't actually a copper bet

Here's the thought most people have next: *"Copper's rising? Then buy cable stocks."*

But that link works differently than you'd expect. **Copper is over 60% of a cable maker's raw-material cost.** Common sense says rising input costs should hurt them. So what actually happened?

![How rising copper prices pass through to cable makers' revenue via escalation clauses](copper-passthrough.png)

Korean cable makers write **cost-escalation clauses** into their contracts: when the copper price rises, that increase is reflected in the selling price. So when copper jumps, margins are defended and **revenue actually grows.** Through copper's 2025 run-up, LS Cable posted 7.6 trillion won in revenue (+12.2%), Taihan Cable 3.6 trillion (+10.5%), and Gaon Cable 2.5 trillion (+47.4%) — all three up.

It isn't free, of course. Pass-through comes with a **lag**, so margins can compress in the first quarter of a spike, and when copper falls, revenue falls with it.

The point is this: **buying a cable stock is not betting on the copper price.** Copper is mostly a pass-through item. What actually separates the winners is **what they sell (product mix) and how much is queued up (order backlog).**

## So what should you watch — the Korean cable map

![Map of Korean cable companies — LS Cable, Gaon, Taihan, Iljin Electric, LS Eco Energy](korea-cable-map.png)

- **LS Cable & System**: top-four globally in submarine cable and HVDC. It is building one of the world's largest submarine-cable plants in Virginia for roughly 1 trillion won, targeting completion in H2 2027 and commercial output in Q1 2028. **Note it is unlisted** — direct investment isn't possible, so exposure typically comes through listed affiliates (LS Corp, LS Electric, Gaon Cable, LS Eco Energy).
- **Gaon Cable**: an LS Cable subsidiary making **busducts** — the heavy power trunks that distribute electricity inside a data center. It has won up to 6 trillion won cumulatively from Big Tech including Meta. This is the most direct AI linkage in the group.
- **Taihan Cable**: extra-high-voltage underground and submarine cable. Q1 2026 revenue of 1.083 trillion won (+26.6%) and operating profit of 60.4 billion won (+122.9%), with a record 3.83 trillion won backlog. But the valuation is stretched (more below).
- **Iljin Electric**: extra-high-voltage transformers and power equipment. Backlog around 2.6 trillion won.
- **LS Eco Energy**: Vietnam production base. Q1 2026 extra-high-voltage cable revenue jumped **177%.**

And the **real moat here is not copper but the technical threshold.** Only five companies worldwide can make 525kV extra-high-voltage HVDC submarine cable — Prysmian (Italy), Nexans (France), NKT (Denmark), Sumitomo (Japan) and LS Cable — and together they hold over 85% of the market. The submarine cable market is expected to grow from $20 billion in 2025 to $33.8 billion by 2030, about 11% a year. Anyone can buy copper; not anyone can make 525kV.

## Investor's view — four points

**① Don't buy cable stocks off the copper chart.** Copper is largely passed through. Trading cable names on the copper price gets the causality backwards.

**② Watch the product mix.** Within the same "cable company," commodity wire and 525kV submarine cable have completely different margins. The company raising its high-value share wins.

**③ Valuation dispersion is extreme.** Taihan Cable trades at a 2026 forward P/E of **133x** and 6x book — stretched enough that brokerages say so explicitly — while Iljin Electric sits at **18.8x**, below its peer average of 30–33x. Same theme, wildly different prices. The question from [Episode 4](/en/p/power-supercycle-or-bubble/) — has the valuation run ahead of the earnings? — has to be asked stock by stock.

**④ Completion date = earnings date.** LS Cable's Virginia plant targets Q1 2028; Taihan's second Dangjin submarine plant targets 2027. Today's backlog turns into revenue only then — a gap of nearly two years between expectation and results.

## Summary

- **Data centers use under 2% of world copper demand.** The price still rose because AI created about a **quarter of 2026's demand growth** (roughly 110,000 tonnes), which is comparable to this year's entire 150,000-tonne shortfall. When inventories are empty, the last tonne sets the price.
- **Supply takes 18 years.** An average 17.9 years from discovery to production, 5–10 of them just permitting, compounded by falling ore grades and the Grasberg accident (591,000 tonnes lost). Yet Goldman Sachs alone forecasts a surplus, and aluminum substitution and rising scrap supply are real counterarguments.
- **Cable stocks are not a copper bet.** Escalation clauses pass copper through. The real drivers are **product mix (525kV submarine cable, busducts) and backlog**, and the real moat is a **technical threshold only five companies clear.**

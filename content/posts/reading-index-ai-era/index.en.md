This is the last episode. Through [Episode 5](/en/p/how-to-pick-ai-etf/) we learned to open up a product. Now it's time to **open up a number.**

You've seen headlines like this: "Brokerage X sets KOSPI target at 9,300." At first glance it reads like fortune telling. How does anyone pin next year's index to a single figure?

That number isn't a prophecy. **It's a multiplication.** And once you know that, your whole relationship with forecasts changes.

## How a target index gets built

The formula is startlingly simple.

![A brokerage target index is 12-month forward EPS multiplied by a target PER](forecast-is-multiplication.png)

**Target index = 12-month forward EPS × target PER**

A real example. In January 2026, Korea Investment & Securities put the KOSPI's 12-month forward EPS at **435 points**, applied a fair PER of **13x**, and published a target of **5,650**. 435 × 13 = 5,655. That's the whole thing.

Two terms, briefly:

- **12-month forward EPS**: the earnings per share KOSPI-listed companies are **estimated** to produce over the coming year — the average of analyst estimates, i.e. the consensus.
- **Target PER**: how many times those earnings the market will pay. Justified with historical bands, comparisons to other markets, ROE, and so on.

It's the same structure we dissected for individual target prices in [Semiconductor Episode 9](/en/p/target-price-anatomy/). Only the subject changed from a stock to an index.

One thing matters here. **A forecast can change for exactly two reasons:** the earnings estimate moved, or the multiple the market assigns moved. So "why did they cut the target?" always translates to "did they cut EPS, or did they cut PER?" Those are entirely different stories.

## In 2026, the two numbers moved in opposite directions

This is the real story of the year.

![KOSPI 12-month forward EPS tripled while the forward PER was halved](eps-vs-per.png)

Start with **EPS**. It was 435 points on January 6 — and that was already 28.8% above the October 2025 view. It kept going: roughly 660 points at the end of March, **1,056.4 points** on June 15. About triple in seven months.

Now **PER**. Based on the April 10 close, Daishin Securities had it at 7.19x and Eugene Investment at 7.1x. On June 12 it was 8.1x. And at the end of July, Kim Dae-jun of Korea Investment & Securities put it at **5.3x** in an August 4 report.

Put those side by side and something looks off. The index went from around 4,300 in January to over 9,000 in June, yet the multiple fell. Kim Hak-kyun, head of research at Shinyoung Securities, framed it precisely: **"When the KOSPI was around 2300, the market's average PER was about 9.5x. Recently the index has risen sharply, yet the PER has actually fallen to 8.1x."**

The reason is simple. **Earnings estimates rose faster than prices did.**

The result is that the KOSPI's forward PER is now below where it sat in past crises. Roughly 10x during the 1997 currency crisis, 7.8–8.1x in the 2008 financial crisis, about 7.7x during the 2024 impeachment turmoil. Today it's in the 5s.

## Here's where most people reach the same conclusion

"So it's incredibly cheap, right?"

A natural reaction. And **I think this is the most important point in this episode.**

A low PER reads two ways:

1. The market **believes** these earnings and the price is low → undervalued
2. The market **doesn't believe** these earnings → fairly priced

Same 5.3x, opposite meanings. And there's a remark that tells us which one we're in. Roh Dong-gil of Shinhan Securities, in early August:

> "The recent correction is not a correction in earnings but in confidence in the earnings outlook" — **"The market believes only 60% of the current consensus."**

Believing 60% of the consensus means the market isn't really applying 5.3x to an EPS of 1,244. It's closer to applying 8–9x to an effective EPS of 700–800. The calculator prints 5.3x; the market's head is somewhere else.

What brokerages are actually doing confirms it. When Daishin cut its target from 11,500 to **9,300** on August 3, the stated reason was **lowering the semiconductor target PER from 8x to 7x**. They didn't touch EPS — they cut the multiple. Shinhan went from 11,000 to 8,800 on July 30.

**A PER is less a price tag than a confidence reading.** If the multiple shrinks as the earnings estimate inflates, that isn't the market getting cheaper — it may be the market discounting the estimate.

## Cyclical industries look cheapest when earnings are best

And none of this is new. Memory has always behaved this way.

![Samsung Electronics posted record profit in 2018, but its share price peaked in November 2017](cycle-trap.png)

Look at the road Samsung Electronics actually travelled.

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| November 2017 | All-time high share price (about 2.87m won, pre-split) |
| 2018 | Operating profit **58.89 trillion won** — a record at the time |
| 2019 | Operating profit **27.77 trillion won** — **down 52.8%** |

**The price peaked more than a year before earnings did.** Through all of 2018, as profit climbed toward a record, the stock was already falling. And at that point the PER was near historic lows.

The mechanism: at the top of a cycle, profits explode. The denominator swells, so the PER looks small. But the market knows those profits won't hold, so it assigns a low multiple. **It isn't cheap-looking — it's earnings that deserve to be priced cheaply.**

Kim Hak-kyun's warning lands right here: **"With semiconductors, the first thing to watch out for is the moment upward earnings revisions stop."** And then: **"In the past, once results turned, the estimates themselves collapsed to an absurd degree."**

That's not hyperbole. Samsung's semiconductor division lost roughly 15 trillion won in 2023 alone. Three years later it earns 89 trillion in a single quarter. That amplitude is memory — the cycle we covered in [Semiconductor Episode 4](/en/p/semiconductor-supercycle/).

## Why this is the whole index's problem

Here [Episode 1](/en/p/what-is-kospi-index/) and [Episode 2](/en/p/index-concentration-trap/) come back around.

![Semiconductors are 65% of KOSPI operating profit, and 99.7% of Samsung Electronics' profit](what-kospi-earnings-are.png)

- **Semiconductors account for 65% of total operating profit** across KOSPI-listed companies (as tallied in April 2026). Heo Jae-hwan of Eugene Investment put it in the high 60s in a June report and estimated that **stripping out semiconductors leaves the KOSPI around 4,100–4,200.**
- The same holds inside a single company. Of Samsung Electronics' 89.5 trillion won in second-quarter 2026 operating profit, the semiconductor (DS) division contributed 89.2 trillion — **99.7%**. The DX division, which covers phones and appliances, booked 48 trillion in revenue and an **operating loss of 800 billion won, its first ever.**

So the chain looks like this:

**KOSPI index forecast → KOSPI EPS forecast → semiconductor earnings forecast → DRAM contract prices and HBM volumes**

You're talking about an index, but you're really talking about memory prices. Agreeing with a brokerage's KOSPI target means, in practice, **agreeing with its memory cycle forecast.** Knowing that changes how you read a forecast article.

## So how accurate are forecasts?

Honestly: not very. This year is the proof.

In January 2026, brokerage KOSPI bands mostly ran **3,500 to 5,650**. Kiwoom and iM at 3,500–4,500, Hanwha 3,700–4,500, Shinhan 3,700–5,000, KB 3,800–5,000, Samsung 4,000–4,900, Daishin 4,000–5,300, Yuanta 4,200–5,200, Hyundai Motor up to 5,500, Korea Investment 5,650.

Here's what actually happened.

| When | KOSPI |
|---|---|
| January 2 | 4,309.65 |
| June 18 | 9,063.84 (first close above 9,000) |
| June 19 | 9,385.59 intraday |
| July 28 | 6,023.66 (-10.84%) |
| July 29 | 5,663.24 (second straight circuit breaker) |
| July 31 | 6,595.45 (+17.91%) |
| August 10 | 6,299.66 |

**Even the most aggressive forecast — 5,650 — was about 60% of the June high.** And a month later two-thirds of that high was gone. That was the week covered in [Episode 3](/en/p/circuit-breaker-week/) and [Episode 4](/en/p/leverage-etf-trap/).

Views remain split. Domestic houses are lowering the bar (Daishin 9,300, Shinhan 8,800); 828 target-price downgrade reports landed in July alone, with nearly 200 more in August. Foreign banks are holding: Goldman Sachs at 12,000, JP Morgan at 12,500, Morgan Stanley at 9,000 — and Morgan Stanley actually upgraded from neutral to overweight.

**The same market yields 12,500 and 8,800 at once.** That's less about who's wrong than about which assumption went into which of the two variables. It's why the assumptions matter more than the number.

## How to check it yourself — plus one common trap

- **KRX Information Data System** (data.krx.co.kr) → Statistics → Index → PER/PBR/dividend yield. Official PER and PBR figures for the KOSPI index.
- **The trap**: what you see there is **trailing** PER, computed from the most recent reported results. It is not the "12-month forward PER" you read about in the news. When earnings are surging, trailing PER runs much higher. Mixing the two produces nonsense conclusions.
- **Forward PER has no official statistic.** It's a consensus of analyst estimates compiled by data vendors like FnGuide and QuantiWise; individuals usually find it in the valuation band charts of research PDFs that brokerages publish free.
- Values differ slightly by house on the same date (7.19x at Daishin, 7.1x at Eugene, both on the April 10 close), because each compiles a different set of estimates. **It's a number for direction and level, not for decimal places.**

## Summary

- **A target index is multiplication, not prophecy.** 12-month forward EPS × target PER. When a forecast changes, ask which of the two moved.
- **In 2026 the two moved opposite ways.** EPS roughly tripled from 435p to over 1,000p, while PER fell from 7–8x to 5.3x — below past crisis levels.
- **A low PER does not automatically mean cheap.** If the market doesn't believe the earnings estimate, it assigns a low multiple. Hence the diagnosis that the market "believes only 60% of the consensus."
- **Cyclicals look cheapest at peak earnings.** Samsung set a record profit in 2018, but the stock peaked in November 2017, and 2019 profit halved (-52.8%).
- **And semiconductors are 65% of KOSPI earnings today.** Which makes a KOSPI forecast, in substance, a memory cycle forecast.

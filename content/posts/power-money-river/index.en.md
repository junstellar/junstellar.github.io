In [Episode 6](/en/p/ai-money-flow/) of the semiconductor series, I traced how "my ChatGPT subscription becomes SK Hynix's earnings" — a **river of money** flowing from Big Tech capex through Nvidia, TSMC and Hynix. Today I do the same for power: following the money that Big Tech and utilities pour in, all the way to whichever company's earnings it becomes.

But once you draw it, the river of electric money has a **completely different shape** from semiconductors.

## Semiconductors are one river; power is a delta

The semiconductor money was essentially **one big river**: Big Tech capex → Nvidia (GPU design) → TSMC (foundry) → Hynix, ASML. Narrow, deep, and guarded by a near-monopolist at every turn. That's why Nvidia alone soaked up most of the flow.

Electric money splits like a **delta**. Money leaving the power plant scatters into four branches — generation → transmission → distribution → cooling — becoming the earnings of many companies. There is no single "Nvidia." That's the defining feature of power investing, and also why the stocks feel so confusing.

![The river of electric money — capital splitting into four branches from power plant to GPU](money-delta.png)

## 1. Where the money comes from — the river's source

The river has two headwaters.

**① Big Tech's data-center capex.** McKinsey estimates **$6.7 trillion** will pour into data centers by 2030 (over $1.7T of that is infrastructure excluding IT gear). It's the "AI grows by eating electricity" theme from [Episode 1](/en/p/ai-power-hunger/), translated into the language of money.

**② Utilities' capital spending.** This is the heart of today's episode. S&P Global forecasts **~$1.3 trillion** in combined capex for 46 US utilities over 2026–2030 — an all-time high, and a **29% jump** in a single year from 2025 (~$200 billion). They're rebuilding the grid wholesale to handle the power demand data centers created.

These two streams merge, then split into generation, transmission, distribution and cooling. The global leaders in the chart: generation is GE Vernova ($176B backlog, a 116GW gas-turbine order book); transmission is the transformer market ($26B/year, 3–5 year lead times); cooling is the just-emerging liquid-cooling market ($3B in 2026 → $7B by 2029).

## 2. In every data-center dollar, how much is power?

So of every dollar going into a data center, how much is power's share? Interestingly, **the answer depends on your vantage point.**

![Power's share of a data-center dollar — total-cost view vs. new-equipment-order view](dollar-split.png)

By **total cost of ownership (depreciation-adjusted)**, servers and GPUs are still the biggest at 60%, with power and facilities at 40%. That's the picture from semiconductor Episode 6, where "the GPU drinks the whole river."

But look at the **flow of newly ordered equipment** and the story changes. Electrical gear is about 40% and cooling about 10% — power and cooling infrastructure climb to nearly half. The GPUs are already made and sold by Nvidia; the power infrastructure to run them is only now being scrambled together. In other words, **the further downstream you go (transformers, switchgear, cooling), the faster power's share grows** — the decisive way the power river differs from semiconductors.

One number captures it: the power a single rack draws jumped from 8kW in 2024 → 15–27kW in 2026 → **over 120kW** for Nvidia's GB200 rack. The denser the GPUs, the more equipment is needed — several times over — to handle their heat and electricity.

## 3. So how much reached Korean stocks?

How much of the river has actually landed in Korean companies' accounts shows up in **order backlogs** (how to read a backlog gets its own Episode 10). The combined Q1 2026 backlog of Korea's big three power-equipment makers **topped 32 trillion won** — at least four to five years of work.

![Backlog of Korea's three power-equipment makers — Hyosung 15.1T, HD Hyundai Electric 11.9T, LS Electric 5.6T won](korea-backlog.png)

- **Transmission is hottest.** Hyosung Heavy 15.1T won, HD Hyundai Electric 11.9T won — the result of extra-high-voltage transformers creating 3–5 year queues in the US (Episode 3 asked "why HD Hyundai Electric rose as much as a chip stock").
- **Distribution is the second wave.** LS Electric 5.6T won. Cumulative switchgear orders from North American Big Tech data centers passed 1 trillion won and are accelerating through 2026.
- **Generation:** Doosan Enerbility, with a 2026 order target of 13.4T won (gas turbines and SMRs), is winning US Big Tech orders — fitting for the star of [Episode 2](/en/p/smr-nuclear-ai/).
- **Cooling** is still early. Samsung (FläktGroup), GST and SK Enmove are moving, but mostly at PoC and validation stage; the multi-hundred-billion-won real orders remain the next wave.

## Investor's view — three ways to read the delta

**① The gains are dispersed.** With semiconductors, watching Nvidia alone showed you 80% of the river; with power, you have to watch all four branches — generation, transmission, distribution, cooling — for the full picture. That no single stock dominates also means, flipped around, that **many companies boom at once.**

**② The bottleneck is "lead time," not "technology."** The semiconductor bottleneck was cutting-edge process capability. The power bottleneck is physical capacity — transformers on 3–5 year lead times, switchgear essentially sold out through 2028. It's a **"there's money but no product"** structure, which favors companies that already banked a backlog.

**③ The bottleneck moves.** Right now transmission (transformers) is hottest, but once that eases, attention shifts to the next chokepoint — distribution, then cooling. You need the value-chain map from [Episode 5](/en/p/power-value-chain-map/) to follow where the money flows next.

## Summary

- Unlike the single semiconductor river, electric money flows as a **four-branch delta: generation, transmission, distribution, cooling.** So the beneficiary stocks are far more numerous and dispersed.
- The source is Big Tech data-center capex ($6.7T by 2030) and utility capex ($1.3T in the US). What differs from semiconductors is that **power's share grows the further downstream you go.**
- It has already reached Korea: 32T won in combined backlog for the big three (Hyosung, HD Hyundai Electric, LS Electric); generation is Doosan; cooling still early. The bottleneck is "lead time," and it **migrates** from transmission → distribution → cooling.

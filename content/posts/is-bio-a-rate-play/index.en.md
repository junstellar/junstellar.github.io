[Episode 3](/en/p/why-shipbuilding-defense-rise/) covered shipbuilding and defence — one with a channel opening into AI, the other standing on entirely different ground.

Now for the real star of August's KOSDAQ rally: **biotech.** The KOSDAQ 150 Healthcare index rose **34.66%** between July 31 and August 10, roughly double the 18.72% gain in the broader KOSDAQ.

Biotech comes with an old formula attached: **"biotech is a function of interest rates."** The past two days tested that formula head-on, and the result wasn't what the formula predicted.

## The textbook first — why biotech is said to be rate-sensitive

![Duration theory and the order of 2026's cyclical peaks](duration-textbook.png)

The concept is **duration.** It comes from bonds, but it applies to equities too.

Say a drug takes ten years to reach the market. The rate you use to convert that future money into today's value is the interest rate — and **the further out the cash flow, the harder the discount bites.** A small rise in rates takes a big bite out of profits ten years away. A company earning money this year loses much less.

There's a second effect. Kang Hyun-ki of DB Financial Investment puts it plainly:

> **"Pharmaceutical and biotech firms need to raise large amounts of capital through the drug development process, so when rates rise their funding costs climb."**

Future profits get discounted harder, and the money needed to survive until then gets more expensive. Doubly disadvantaged.

**And in the first half of 2026, the theory held perfectly.** As market rates began climbing at the end of February, cyclical peaks formed in this order:

| Cyclical peak | Date |
|---|---|
| KOSDAQ | April 27 |
| Autos | June 1 |
| Semiconductors | June 22 |

**The assets selling the most distant future rolled over first.** Textbook.

## Then came August 19

Last week the US 30-year Treasury yield jumped past **5.3%** — a 19-year high. Korea's 30-year government bond hit 4.751%, an all-time high.

By the textbook, biotech and the KOSDAQ should have taken the worst of it. Here's what happened.

![On August 19 and 20 the KOSPI and KOSDAQ moved in wildly different magnitudes](two-days-reversed.png)

**August 19 (yields spike)**

| | Change |
|---|---|
| KOSPI | **-5.80%** (6,471.17) |
| KOSDAQ | **-1.17%** (824.46) |
| Samsung Electronics | -7.82% |
| SK Hynix | -9.75% |

The KOSPI fell more than 6% early on, triggering the **25th sell-side sidecar of the year.** Yet **the KOSDAQ fell only one-fifth as much.**

The flows are stranger still. That day foreigners bought a net **46.3 billion won** and institutions **45.8 billion won** of KOSDAQ stocks. Alteogen, the largest KOSDAQ name, closed higher.

The next day mirrored it. On **August 20**, with the US Treasury expanding buybacks and SK Hynix announcing a 40 trillion won share cancellation, yields calmed and the KOSPI rebounded **+5.89%** (6,852.58) — while the KOSDAQ managed only **+1.99%** (840.89). A buy-side sidecar triggered at 9:57 a.m., one day after the sell-side one.

**Falling or rising, the thing that reacted violently to rates was semiconductors, not biotech.**

## So why did it invert?

The easy conclusion is "the textbook was wrong." Dig further and it isn't.

![An asset that already fell falls less in the next shock](why-reversed.png)

**First, the KOSDAQ had already taken its hit.** The table above is the evidence: the KOSDAQ peaked on April 27 and fell continuously through July — down 21.44% in July alone ([Episode 2](/en/p/when-the-leader-rests/)). **It had already passed through its rate-sensitive stretch.**

**Second, there was nobody left to sell.** The number from Episode 2: KOSDAQ margin loans fell from 11.05 trillion won at the end of April to 5.83 trillion in early August, down 47.2%. The must-sell supply was already gone.

**Third, semiconductors had just run.** Between August 12 and 18 the KOSPI surged toward 7,200. The side with more to give back was semiconductors, not the KOSDAQ.

Which leaves this:

> **What duration tells you** — the order in which things respond when rates move
> **What duration doesn't tell you** — how far any one of them falls today

**An asset that fell first falls less in the next shock.** That doesn't contradict the textbook; it applies the textbook one step further. Kang followed exactly that logic to his next call: if rates calm, the rebound comes in reverse order. On August 11 he wrote that **"from here you should watch autos and semiconductors for a rebound rather than the KOSDAQ."** Semiconductors led the index from August 12. He was right.

## So what does set biotech's price?

If not rates, then what? Here biotech's peculiarity shows up.

![The earnings structure and valuation methods of biotech companies](bio-earnings.png)

**Of 75 KOSDAQ pharma and biotech companies, 45 posted an operating profit in the first half of 2026 and 30 posted a loss.** Four in ten are still losing money — **which means you cannot compute a PER for them at all.**

Even where you can, the numbers are unusual. Alteogen trades at a **PER of 169** (as of August 14, the largest KOSDAQ stock by value). LigaChem Biosciences, worth some 5 trillion won, has no PER because it's lossmaking.

Which is why the sector has coined **PDR** — Price to Dream Ratio. It sounds like a joke and it gets used seriously: value set by the future worth of a development pipeline rather than by current profit.

**But concluding "so it's a rally built on hope alone" would also be wrong.**

Korean biotech disclosed **8 licensing-out deals worth roughly $8.67 billion (about 13.45 trillion won)** in the first half of 2026 — already more than half of last year's full-year total. The largest was Aribio's $4.7 billion Alzheimer's deal with China's Fosun Pharma; Alteogen signed $285 million with a GSK subsidiary in January and $579 million with Biogen in March. Alteogen's first-quarter operating margin was 54.9%.

The wider KOSDAQ tells the same story. First-half operating profit rose **61.54% to 9.43 trillion won**, and the number of profitable companies grew by 121 to 800.

**Earnings did improve. Whether that's why these particular stocks rose in August is a separate question.**

## Which names had a catalyst, and which didn't

Open August's biotech gainers one by one and they split.

- **Alteogen** had a clear, specific catalyst. On August 7, BlackRock disclosed a **5.03% stake (2,694,448 shares)** — crossing the threshold from 4.98% the previous week, filed as a simple investment. The stock jumped double digits that day and pulled the whole KOSDAQ with it.
- **Most of the rest** had no new August catalyst I could find. HLB refiled rivoceranib with the FDA in July, but no approval news came in August — and on August 19 it fell 2.43%, worse than Alteogen. ABL Bio and LigaChem Biosciences had no confirmed new major deals in August either.

In other words, **one stock's catalyst lifted a whole sector.** On top of that came money displaced by the single-stock leverage ETF restrictions covered in [Index Episode 4](/en/p/leverage-etf-trap/), flowing into KOSDAQ cash equities.

One more thing worth noting. Among August's gainers is a company that was badly shaken four months earlier over disclosure credibility: Samchundang Pharm was **designated an unfaithful disclosure corporation** by the Korea Exchange at the end of March, and the episode prompted the Financial Supervisory Service to begin work on improving pharma and biotech disclosure. **The more a sector moves on news, the more the reliability of that news has to be part of the analysis.**

## And one thing that pushed those yields up was AI

Finally, the connection I found most interesting in this episode.

Several things drove long yields up in August: a widening US deficit (the CBO raised its annual projection to $2.1 trillion), oil on Middle East tensions, spillover from surging Japanese yields. And one more.

![AI companies' bond issuance is absorbing the bond market's money](ai-through-rates.png)

**Bond issuance by AI companies.**

Corporate bond issuance by firms like Amazon, Meta, Nvidia, Oracle and Alphabet went from $397 billion in 2024 to $1.447 trillion in 2025, and **$2.183 trillion** so far in 2026. Their share of the US investment-grade corporate bond market rose from **2.4% to 16.4%.**

Bank of America argued that AI-related borrowing is crowding out demand for long-dated Treasuries, with corporate bonds and mortgage-backed securities accounting for roughly 0.3 percentage points of this year's rise in the 10-year yield. The press called it a **"reversed crowding-out effect"** — normally governments crowd out corporations; here corporations are crowding out government bonds.

**Money borrowed to build AI data centres lifts long-term yields, and that yield becomes the discount rate for biotech, which appears to have nothing to do with AI.**

Episode 3 said shipbuilding connects to AI directly and defence not at all. Add biotech and the picture completes.

| | Relationship to AI |
|---|---|
| Shipbuilding | **Direct** — sells power equipment to data centres |
| Defence | **None** — geopolitics is the whole story |
| Biotech | **Indirect** — AI-driven yields become its discount rate |

Three different ways of showing why "the not-AI things" was never a coherent bundle.

## Summary

- **Biotech is genuinely rate-sensitive.** Profits sit far in the future and the company must keep borrowing until then — doubly exposed. The 2026 peak sequence (KOSDAQ 4/27 → autos 6/1 → semiconductors 6/22) proves it.
- **Yet August 19 inverted it.** On the day yields hit a 19-year high the KOSPI fell 5.80% and the KOSDAQ 1.17%, with foreigners and institutions net buyers of KOSDAQ stocks.
- **The textbook wasn't wrong; the sequence had already run.** The KOSDAQ rolled over in April and its margin debt had halved, leaving nothing forced to sell. **An asset that fell first falls less in the next shock.**
- **Biotech isn't priced on earnings.** Thirty of 75 companies are lossmaking, so PER can't be computed; the sector uses "PDR" instead. But the 8 licensing deals worth $8.67 billion in the first half were real.
- **And one of the things lifting those yields was AI** — AI issuers now account for 16.4% of the US investment-grade bond market, crowding out Treasury demand.

[Episode 5] is this series' killer. **Why retail investors ended up on the wrong side again.** On August 10, as the KOSDAQ surged 7%, individuals sold 672.9 billion won. On August 19, as the KOSPI collapsed 5.8%, they bought 5.64 trillion. And when it rebounded the next day, they sold over 2 trillion. Why the same pattern keeps repeating.

Across the last 27 posts I followed where AI money travels. [Semiconductors](/en/p/what-is-hbm/) were the components, [power](/en/p/ai-power-hunger/) was the fuel, and the [index](/en/p/what-is-kospi-index/) was the whole market AI had swallowed. This series picks up the next question: **where is that money moving now?**

The past two weeks have been exactly that scene. The word "rotation" suddenly filled the Korean financial press in August. So let's open the word up first.

## What sector rotation means

Picture a party. People drift from room to room. One room empties out and the next gets loud. When that happens in a stock market, we call it rotation.

Formally: **after one sector or stock runs, buying moves on to the next in sequence, and the advance continues by relay.**

![The three forms of rotation and the premise the theory rests on](rotation-meaning.png)

It usually comes in three forms.

- **Across sectors** — semiconductors rest, shipbuilders rise; shipbuilders rest, biotech rises
- **Across price tiers** — high-priced names first, then mid-priced, then low-priced
- **Across themes** — AI to robotics, robotics to quantum computing

There's an old body of theory behind this. Sam Stovall systematized it in the 1990s by analysing sector performance across every business cycle since 1945: different sectors lead in different phases. Merrill Lynch's "investment clock" belongs to the same family.

But all of these rest on one shared premise, and I think it's the most important thing in this post.

> **"Money doesn't leave the market entirely — it moves between sectors."**

The pie stays the same size; only the slices change hands. That's what the word "rotation" means.

**In Korea this August, that premise did not hold.** That's the point of this post.

## First, what actually happened in August

![The KOSDAQ and KOSPI split in opposite directions in August](august-decoupling.png)

The KOSDAQ finished July at 719.76 and closed August 11 at 857.84 — **+19.18%**. Over the same stretch the KOSPI was **-3.79%**.

The split has a clear start date: **August 3.** The KOSPI dropped 5.12% that day to 6,257.45, while the KOSDAQ rose 2.44% to 737.35. Then August 4 +5.88% (780.72), August 10 **+6.97%** (854.47), August 11 +0.39% (857.84).

August 10 was the dramatic one. **1,431 KOSDAQ stocks rose** — over 80% of the market — with 11 hitting the daily limit. At 9:50 a.m., KOSDAQ 150 futures and the index held gains of 6.05% and 6.25% for more than a minute, triggering a **buy-side sidecar** — the third of the month (August 3, 4 and 10).

Biotech, robotics and semiconductor suppliers led. The KOSDAQ 150 Healthcare index gained close to 35% in August alone. Alteogen, the largest KOSDAQ stock by value, jumped over 10% after BlackRock disclosed a 5.03% stake (2,694,448 shares) on August 7.

The backdrop is clear too: Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix fell from 53.2% of market capitalisation in July to 51.16% on August 1. The concentration covered in [Index Episode 2](/en/p/index-concentration-trap/) loosened slightly.

Stop here and it's textbook rotation. Money left semiconductors and went to the KOSDAQ.

## Except the money didn't grow

Here's the heart of it. **I checked whether "the money moved" is actually true — by looking at the total.**

![Investor deposits, margin loans and turnover all fell sharply](money-shrank.png)

**Investor deposits** — cash sitting in brokerage accounts waiting to buy. Per the Korea Financial Investment Association, they stood at **97.93 trillion won** on August 11. From the record 139.69 trillion on June 4, that's **41.77 trillion won gone, down 29.9%.** It's the first reading below 100 trillion since February 13. Even against the previous day (100.72 trillion), 2.79 trillion vanished in twenty-four hours.

**Margin loan balances** — money borrowed to buy. After peaking at a record 38.6 trillion won on June 24, the balance collapsed to 28.94 trillion by July 31 and sat at 29.23 trillion on August 7. For the KOSDAQ alone it's starker: 11.05 trillion at the end of April down to 5.83 trillion on August 3 — **less than half (-47.2%).**

**Turnover.** KOSDAQ daily average turnover peaked at 15.57 trillion won in May, fell to 6.12 trillion in July, and ran at 6.19 trillion in August — under half the 10–15 trillion range of the first half.

Put simply:

**Cash on the sidelines fell 30%, and the KOSDAQ rose 19%.**

It didn't rise because money grew. **Shrinking money crowded into a narrower space.** Back to the party: it looks like people are drifting between rooms, but more of them are leaving the building — and the ones who stayed are packed into one small room, which is why that room is loud.

## Where did the money that left actually go?

Some retreated to the waiting room. In July alone, **27.87 trillion won** flowed into money market funds — five times the first-half monthly average.

Some left the country. Between July 1 and 23, Korean investors bought **about 3.71 trillion won** of US stocks net, **four times** the prior month's 928.4 billion. The single largest net purchase was an ETF tracking three times the daily return of the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index. A market badly burned by domestic semiconductor leverage went out and bought American semiconductor leverage at 3x — an uncomfortable footnote to [Index Episode 4](/en/p/leverage-etf-trap/).

## So what rose — expansion, or re-rating?

The people who watch this market read it the same way the arithmetic does.

![Three analysts describing the rally as re-rating rather than broadening](expansion-or-shift.png)

Kim Min-gyu of KB Securities puts it most precisely:

> **"Other sectors rising while semiconductors rest is less a broadening of the advance than neglected sectors being re-rated while the leader takes a break."**

Cho Jun-gi of SK Securities marks the limit:

> **"Rotation into non-semiconductors can generate excess returns, but it does not have the capacity to offset the index decline caused by semiconductor weakness."**

Lee Kyung-min of Daishin Securities adds another angle. The KOSDAQ's high for the year came on April 27 — **two months earlier** than the KOSPI's June 22 peak — so its correction ran longer. What's happening now looks less like an independent re-rating of the KOSDAQ than a **belated catch-up.**

Roh Dong-gil of Shinhan Securities goes one layer deeper: forced liquidation of margin positions removed the supply that *had* to be sold, easing downside pressure. The market rose not because new buying arrived, but because selling stopped.

Three people saying the same thing differently: **the pie didn't grow, the seats changed.**

That said, buyers did exist. Institutions bought a net **1.13 trillion won** of KOSDAQ stocks between August 3 and 11, concentrated in semiconductor suppliers (Tes, Simmtech), biotech (LigaChem Biosciences, ABL Bio) and robotics (SPG, Robotis). There were real hands buying. The point is that the money was **not new — it moved from elsewhere inside the market.**

## Rotation's other face

Rotation usually reads as good news: warmth spreading, opportunity widening. Flip it over and the sentence changes. **A new sector rising means somebody sold out of the previous leader.**

![What happened to stocks after they gave up market leadership](past-leaders.png)

Here's the road Korea actually travelled.

| | EcoPro | Kakao |
|---|---|---|
| Era it led | 2023, secondary batteries | 2020–21, "BBIG" |
| Low and high | Jan 2 2023: 105,600 won → Jul 26: **1,539,000 won** | Jun 24 2021 intraday high: **173,000 won** |
| After that | Year-end close 647,000 won (**-58%** from the high) | 30,000–40,000 won range in 2026 (about **-80%**) |

EcoPro halved within the same year. Kakao has not recovered its high in five years. Hanmi Pharmaceutical, up 627% in 2015, fell 18.1% in a single day on one disclosure that a licensing deal had been cancelled.

**A leader that gives up its seat rarely gets it back in the next cycle.** That face belongs in any conversation about rotation.

## How to check whether it's really rotation

Everything sounds like rotation when described in words. Here's how to check with numbers.

**① Investor deposits** — published daily by the Korea Financial Investment Association. Rising deposits alongside a rising index means new money. **Falling deposits while one market rises means movement, or concentration.** August was the latter.

**② ADR (advance-decline ratio)** — typically advancing issues divided by declining issues over 20 days. 100% means equal numbers; **above 120% is considered overheated, below 70% near a bottom.** It tells you whether gains are broad or confined to a handful of names.

**③ Where turnover goes** — not the index, but where money actually changes hands. KOSPI turnover falling while KOSDAQ turnover rises is evidence of movement. But as this month showed, **both can fall at once** — so always check the total too.

**④ Sidecars and limit-up counts** — a thermometer for short-term overheating. Three buy-side sidecars in one month says the crowding was abrupt.

KRX's Information Data System (data.krx.co.kr) has the price and turnover statistics; the Korea Financial Investment Association publishes deposits and margin balances.

## Summary

- **Rotation is buying moving across sectors, price tiers or themes** while the advance continues by relay.
- **The premise underneath it is "money doesn't leave the market."** In Korea this August, that premise broke.
- **Cash on the sidelines fell 30% while the KOSDAQ rose 19%.** Deposits 139.7tn → 97.9tn, margin loans 38.6tn → 29.2tn, KOSDAQ turnover down 60% from its peak. Money didn't grow — **shrinking money crowded into a narrower space.**
- **So this looks less like a broadening advance than a re-rating while the leader rests.** Three research houses reached that reading in three different phrasings.
- **Rotation has a dark face.** A new sector rising means money left the last leader — and neither EcoPro nor Kakao ever recovered its high.

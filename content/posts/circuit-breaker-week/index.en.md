Today we watch that structure detonate. **The last week of July 2026.**

## What happened over four days

![Daily KOSPI moves from July 28 to 31, 2026](that-week.png)

**Tuesday, July 28.** It broke at the open. At **10:13 a.m.**, with the KOSPI down 8.04%, a **circuit breaker** tripped and trading halted for 20 minutes. It didn't help: the index closed at **−10.84%** (6,023.66). Samsung Electronics fell 13.4%, SK Hynix 14.7%.

The trigger had been pulled the previous day, in China. **CXMT listed in Shanghai on July 27**, surging 465% above its offer price on day one and reaching a market cap of 3.3 trillion yuan (roughly 700 trillion won). The market read it as a frontal challenge to Korea's memory dominance.

**Wednesday, July 29.** This time SK Hynix's results landed just before the open. Q2 operating profit of **60.54 trillion won** — nearly 4 trillion short of the 63.66 trillion consensus. On the 9 a.m. conference call, citing US ADR offering restrictions, management gave no clear answer on shareholder returns or guidance.

At **12:32 p.m.** the circuit breaker tripped again — **the first back-to-back trigger in history**, and the first time both the KOSPI and KOSDAQ halted on consecutive days. The volatility index (VKOSPI) spiked to **97.99** intraday, an all-time high.

**Thursday, July 30.** The index rallied as much as 4.8% intraday before giving it all back to close down 1.23%. In hindsight this was **the bottom.**

**Friday, July 31.** Then the record came in the other direction. A buy-side sidecar tripped just six minutes after the open, at **9:06 a.m.**, and the KOSPI closed up **17.91% (1,001.89 points)** at 6,595.45 — **the largest percentage gain and largest point gain since the index began in 1980.** SK Hynix hit its **daily limit** (+29.95%) around 2:10 p.m., its first limit-up since the price band widened to ±30% in 2015. Samsung Electronics rose 26.81%.

What changed? Overnight, **Microsoft** reported: Azure revenue passed $100 billion in a quarter for the first time, with growth accelerating to 43%. **Alphabet** actually raised its capex guidance. The fear that AI investment was rolling over reversed in a single session.

A three-day, 17.2% decline gave back **86% of itself in one day.**

## So what exactly is a circuit breaker?

It's a familiar headline word, but few people know the actual rules.

![The three levels of circuit breaker triggers and what each does](circuit-breaker-rules.png)

It trips when the index falls a set amount from the prior close **and stays there for one minute.**

- **Level 1 (−8%)**: 20-minute full halt, then 10 minutes of single-price auction trading
- **Level 2 (−15%)**: another 20-minute halt — but it must also be 1 percentage point below where Level 1 triggered
- **Level 3 (−20%)**: the session ends immediately, again requiring 1pp beyond Level 2

A few rules worth knowing: it can trip **only once per day**, and it cannot trigger between 9:00–9:05 a.m. or **after 2:50 p.m.** — halting the tape at the very end serves no purpose. The KOSPI and KOSDAQ use the same thresholds.

The system was introduced in **December 1998**, right after the IMF crisis.

## But is this frequency normal?

Here's the number that defines this episode.

![KOSPI circuit breaker trigger counts and historical trigger dates](cb-history.png)

**In the 28 years from 1998 through 2025, the circuit breaker tripped six times.** In **2026 alone it has tripped nine times.** Of the 15 cumulative triggers as of late July, most occurred this year.

Look at the earlier dates and you see what kind of days these were: April 2000 (dot-com collapse), September 2000 (the aftershock), September 2001 (the day after 9/11), March 2020 (COVID, twice), August 2024 (the yen-carry unwind). **Every one of them is a landmark crisis.**

That has happened nine times this year. July's 33% monthly decline exceeded even the IMF crisis of 1997 (−27%), and against the same month's S&P 500 (−1.7%), Nasdaq (−6.7%) and Nikkei 225 (−11.6%), Korea alone convulsed.

Episode 2 noted the 0.82 correlation between the two dominant stocks. When those two are more than half the index, one semiconductor headline moves the whole market by 8%. **Frequent circuit breakers aren't the cause — they're the symptom.**

## And what are sidecars and VI?

Circuit breakers aren't the only brake. Separating the three makes market news far easier to read.

![How circuit breakers, sidecars and VI differ](three-brakes.png)

- **Circuit breaker** — triggered by the **index**. Halts all trading market-wide.
- **Sidecar** — triggered by **futures prices**. A 5% move in KOSPI 200 futures suspends **program-trading orders for five minutes only.** Ordinary trading continues. This is what tripped on the morning of July 31. Note that sidecars also trigger **on the way up.**
- **VI (volatility interruption)** — triggered per **individual stock**. That one name switches to single-price auction for two minutes.

There's also the **daily price limit** — the maximum a stock can move in one day, ±30% in Korea. It was ±15% until it widened in **June 2015.** SK Hynix's "limit-up" on July 31 was that +30% ceiling.

Worth noting: **the US has no daily price limit on individual stocks.** A stock can multiply or collapse in a day without any band stopping it. Different markets brake differently.

## Does stopping the market actually help? The other side

One caveat matters here. That circuit breakers rescue markets is not settled — academics have argued about it for nearly 30 years.

**The case for** is straightforward: a forced pause in a crash breaks the panic-selling chain and gives investors time to reassess.

**The case against** is more interesting, and it has a name: the **magnet effect.** As the index approaches −8%, investors sell *faster*, precisely because they fear being locked in when trading halts. The price gets pulled toward the threshold like iron to a magnet.

This isn't only theory. **China introduced circuit breakers on January 1, 2016.** They tripped on the 4th. On the 7th they tripped again less than 30 minutes after the open, ending the session early. Amid accusations that the mechanism was accelerating the selling, Chinese regulators **suspended it on January 8 — four days after launch.** It remains the textbook case for the magnet effect.

## What that week left behind

The records weren't the only residue.

Margin loan balances fell from 38.6 trillion won at end-June to **28.94 trillion won** on July 31 — about 10 trillion, a quarter of all leveraged money, gone. Some was voluntary repayment; much was **forced liquidation**, brokers selling collateral automatically. Forced sales totaled around 800 billion won in July, including **122 billion won on July 31 alone.** Which is to say: even on the day of the record rally, some people were being sold out.

Cumulative forced liquidations this year passed 4 trillion won, and personal rehabilitation filings in the first half reached 81,723 — up 13.2% year on year.

## Investor's view — four points

**① A circuit breaker is an outcome, not a warning.** By the time it trips, the market is already down 8%. Rather than "buy when it halts," ask why this market halts so often.

**② The three-day crash and the one-day melt-up share a cause.** When two stocks are half the index, it swings both ways. The upside record and the volatility bill arrive together.

**③ Leverage dies first in a tape like this.** The index recovered 86% of the drop in a single day — but if you were liquidated before Friday, none of that recovery was yours. Surviving and being *built* to survive are different things.

**④ Safeguards are cushions, not shields.** As China showed, a mechanism meant to stop selling can invite it. Taking more risk because a safety net exists gets the order backwards.

## Summary

- **Records went both ways in one week.** July 28: −10.84% with a circuit breaker. July 29: −5.98%, the first back-to-back trigger ever. July 31: **+17.91%, the largest gain in 46 years** — recovering 86% of the three-day decline in a day.
- **Circuit breakers run −8%/−15%/−20%**, meaning a 20-minute halt, another 20-minute halt, and an early close. Sidecars suspend program trading for five minutes on a 5% futures move; VI switches a single stock to auction for two minutes.
- **Six triggers in 28 years; nine this year alone.** Frequent halts are a symptom of concentration, not its cause. And braking doesn't always help — China abandoned its circuit breaker four days after launching it.

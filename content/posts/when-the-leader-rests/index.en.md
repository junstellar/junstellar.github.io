[Episode 1](/en/p/what-is-sector-rotation/) turned up a strange pair of numbers: investor deposits fell 30% while the KOSDAQ rose 19%. Not because money grew, but because shrinking money crowded into a narrower space.

Which leaves the next question. **Why August, specifically?**

Semiconductors collapsed in July too — far more violently, in fact. Yet July produced no rotation. Working out what differed reveals that the conditions for rotation are much fussier than they look.

## Three faces in six weeks

Line up the three regimes first.

![July fell together, early August decoupled, August 12 reversed](three-regimes.png)

| | July | Aug 1–11 | Aug 12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Semiconductors | Collapse | Still weak | Return |
| KOSPI | **-22.19%** | -3.79% | **+3.68%** |
| KOSDAQ | **-21.44%** | **+19.18%** | +0.12% |

In July the KOSPI went from 8,476.48 to 6,595.45 and the KOSDAQ from 916.18 to 719.76. **They fell almost identically.**

The bottom of that month looked like this. On July 28, as the KOSPI dropped 732.09 points (10.84%), the KOSDAQ fell 7.72% — 1,480 stocks down against 211 up. The VKOSPI volatility index spiked to 83.43. On July 21 the KOSDAQ touched 747.00 intraday for a 52-week low; its market capitalisation had gone from 680 trillion won in April to 420 trillion that day, 260 trillion erased in three months.

**Crashes do not produce rotation.** This is an old property of markets: in violent declines, correlations rise. There's no room to sort good from bad, so whatever *can* be sold gets sold. The week covered in [Index Episode 3](/en/p/circuit-breaker-week/) was exactly that.

## But semiconductors were weak in August too

Here my first hypothesis was wrong. I approached this assuming "semiconductors went flat in August, so other things could rise." Checking the data, that isn't what happened.

**On August 6, SK Hynix fell 10.37% in one day** — closing at 1,495,000 won after touching 1,481,000 intraday, down 11.21%. Samsung Electronics fell 6.30% the same day. The trigger was disappointing guidance from SanDisk in the US. Foreigners sold a net 1.69 trillion won of SK Hynix alone that day.

Cumulatively it's even clearer. Comparing the July 31 close with the August 12 close:

| | July 31 | Aug 12 | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Samsung Electronics | 262,500 won | 255,500 won | **-2.7%** |
| SK Hynix | 1,718,000 won | 1,504,000 won | **-12.5%** |

While the KOSDAQ rose 19%, SK Hynix fell another 12%. **Semiconductors weren't resting — they were still taking hits.**

And yet the KOSDAQ rose through it. So what was different from July?

## The real condition — forced selling ended

The answer is in *who* was doing the selling.

![KOSDAQ margin loan balances fell to less than half their April level](forced-selling-ends.png)

**KOSDAQ margin loan balances fell from 11.05 trillion won at the end of April to 5.83 trillion on August 3 — down 47.2%.**

More than half of the borrowed money was liquidated. This matters because margin calls **don't discriminate between stocks.** Once collateral ratios break, good companies and bad get sold alike. A great deal of July's identical decline across both markets came from that forced selling.

Only after that supply is exhausted does a market start *choosing* again. Roh Dong-gil of Shinhan Securities puts his finger on exactly this point:

> **"Liquidation of margin positions doesn't itself create new buying demand, but the reduction in forced selling pressure eased downside risk."**
>
> **"The current rebound is a normalisation of price-formation conditions rather than an improvement in corporate fundamentals."**

The market rose not because new buying arrived, but because **the people who had to sell were gone.** That's how Episode 1's "money shrank yet the index rose" resolves.

On top of that came the single-stock leverage ETF restrictions covered in [Index Episode 4](/en/p/leverage-etf-trap/). The channel that funnelled money into large-cap semiconductors was narrowed by regulation. Turnover shows how abruptly it closed.

- **KODEX SK Hynix single-stock leverage ETF turnover**: 3.63 trillion won on July 30 → **178.2 billion on August 10 (-95.1%)**
- **SK Hynix turnover**: a record 21.9 trillion won on June 23 → 4.73 trillion on August 10 (about one-fifth)
- **Samsung Electronics turnover**: 15.8 trillion won on June 24 → 3.78 trillion on August 10 (about 24%)

The leverage flow that amplified July's crash **evaporated by 95% in a month.** Samsung and SK Hynix's combined weight in market capitalisation slipped from 53.2% in July to 51.16% on August 1 partly for this reason.

**So the condition for rotation wasn't the leader "resting" — it was forced selling ending.** And that's a fussy condition: the crash has to be over, the liquidation has to be finished, and the leader has to still be absent. A narrow window in between.

## And the KOSDAQ was never a refuge from semiconductors

Go one layer deeper and something interesting appears. **Was what rose in August really "not semiconductors"?**

![Semiconductor names among the KOSDAQ's 100 largest stocks rose 88% in a year](kosdaq-is-semi-too.png)

Among the KOSDAQ's 100 largest companies by market value, semiconductor and related-equipment names went from **17 at the end of July 2025 to 32 in July 2026 — an 88% increase in a year.**

The KOSDAQ had already become substantially semiconductor-based. So when semiconductors break, the KOSDAQ breaks with them. July's joint decline was no coincidence.

The August leaders say the same. Among the stocks institutions bought most on the KOSDAQ between August 3 and 11 were Tes (55.98bn won), Simmtech (50.06bn), Jeju Semiconductor (39.33bn) and TSE (33.89bn) — **semiconductor suppliers, all of them.** Biotech names like LigaChem Biosciences and ABL Bio and robotics names like SPG and Robotis rose too, of course.

So August's advance was less a move *away* from semiconductors than **a move down from large-cap semiconductors to small-cap ones.** "Escape from semiconductors" is only half right.

## Down together, up separately

There are numbers for this asymmetry.

![Days when the KOSPI rose and the KOSDAQ fell hit a record 32 of 132 sessions](asymmetry.png)

Across the 132 trading sessions from January 1 to July 16, 2026, **the KOSPI rose while the KOSDAQ fell on 32 of them — 24%.** That is the **highest annual proportion since the KOSDAQ opened in 1996.** Last year the same figure was 13%, so it jumped 1.8-fold.

The reverse — KOSPI down, KOSDAQ up — happened on just **11 days (8%)**.

Lee Gun-jae, head of KOSDAQ research at IBK Securities, sums up the structure:

> **"Korean semiconductor equipment and materials companies have improved their technology and earnings, but with the KOSPI's volatility widening, investors are concentrating funds into the KOSPI rather than the KOSDAQ."**

**When semiconductors fall the KOSDAQ takes the hit; when semiconductors rise the KOSDAQ gets left out.** That was the default setting of the Korean market in 2026. The first two weeks of August were the exception, not the rule.

## And today, the window closed

The day I'm writing this happened to be the test.

![On August 12 semiconductors surged and August's leading sectors all fell](window-closes.png)

**On August 12, 2026, semiconductors came back.** Samsung Electronics jumped 6.68% to close in the 255,000-won range and SK Hynix rose 5.54% to the 1.5 million-won range, on strength in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and US big-tech earnings.

The result:

- **KOSPI 6,579.04 (+3.68%, +233.51 points)** — the electrical & electronics sector rose 5.92% and carried the index.
- **KOSDAQ 858.91 (+0.12%, +1.07 points)** — essentially flat.

And **the very sectors that had led all month fell.** KOSPI pharmaceuticals -3.53%, KOSDAQ pharmaceuticals -1.88%, KOSDAQ general services -1.95%.

Flows reversed within a day. On the KOSPI, foreigners and institutions both bought while individuals sold over 3 trillion won. On the KOSDAQ it was the mirror image: foreigners sold a net 201.7 billion won and institutions 168.0 billion, while only individuals bought, 376.6 billion.

**That is precisely the inverse of August 10** from [Episode 1](/en/p/what-is-sector-rotation/), when the KOSDAQ surged 6.97% and individuals sold 672.9 billion won. In two sessions the retail position flipped. I'll come back to that in Episode 5.

Lee Kyung-min of Daishin Securities kept his closing comment plain:

> **"Large-cap strength unfolded amid joint net buying by foreigners and institutions. Semiconductors in particular were strong as big-tech earnings lifted sentiment."**

One day's move settles nothing. But **what opens and closes the rotation window** is unusually legible in this single session.

## Will the window reopen?

Views differ.

Na Seung-doo of SK Securities sees the structural conditions in place: **"the KOSDAQ is beginning to satisfy most of the conditions present in past periods when it outperformed the KOSPI — rotation after a leadership rally, delisting of weak companies, and next-generation themes like robotics and biotech."** Policy factors get cited too: pension benchmarks incorporating the KOSDAQ, a KOSDAQ premium index due in September.

Others read it technically. Kwon Beom-seok of Samsung Securities notes the KOSDAQ has fallen to around its **120-month moving average (roughly 800 points)**, and that an index dropping below that line after only three months of decline is rare.

Roh Dong-gil attaches conditions: for the advance to continue, **investment trust and pension buying must persist and small-cap earnings improvement has to be confirmed.** So far price conditions have normalised; earnings have not improved.

The other side isn't settled either. Semiconductor target prices were cut en masse in August. Kiwoom Securities lowered Samsung Electronics from 390,000 to 350,000 won and SK Hynix from 2.2m to 2.1m on August 11 (keeping its buy rating), while NH, Daishin and Samsung Securities cut SK Hynix to 3.4m, 3.2m and 3.0m respectively. The stated reason was mostly **rising memory supply in 2027.** One day of gains didn't lift the bar.

For what it's worth, I think the asymmetry figures above (24% versus 8%) are the most honest answer. **A KOSDAQ that keeps pace while semiconductors rally has been rare this year.** And Nvidia's earnings on August 26 sit close ahead.

## Summary

- **Crashes don't produce rotation.** July: KOSPI -22.19%, KOSDAQ -21.44% — nearly identical, because margin calls don't discriminate between stocks.
- **Semiconductors were weak in August too.** SK Hynix fell 10.37% on August 6 alone. Rotation didn't arrive because the leader "rested."
- **The real condition was the end of forced selling.** KOSDAQ margin loans fell 47.2%, from 11 trillion won in April to under 6 trillion in early August. Once the must-sell supply was gone, the market began choosing again.
- **The KOSDAQ was no refuge from semiconductors.** 32 of its 100 largest names are semiconductor-related (+88% in a year), and semiconductor suppliers topped institutional buying in August.
- **And the window is narrow.** KOSPI-up/KOSDAQ-down days hit a record 24% this year, and on August 12 — the day semiconductors returned — the KOSDAQ stopped at +0.12%.

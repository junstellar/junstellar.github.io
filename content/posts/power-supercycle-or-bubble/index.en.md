It's already been six days since I posted [part 3](/en/p/transformer-supercycle/). Work piled up and I couldn't even open the blog for a while. The market swung again in the meantime. Since I'm back after a break, let me take on the most uncomfortable question I've been putting off in this series.

Nuclear (part 2) and transformers (part 3) both ran hot as "AI power-crunch plays." Yet these stocks fell 30% each over the past few months. So today's question is this: **is this a real ten-year supercycle, or a bubble that inflates and pops like the battery stocks did?** Just as AI-chip part 4 weighed "this time is different," let's put the same scale to power.

## Put the evidence on both sides of the scale

Up front: I don't think either side is simply right. Both are true. Let's lay the evidence side by side.

![Supercycle evidence vs bubble warnings — both sides weighed](supercycle-vs-bubble.png)

**The "real supercycle" side.** The strongest card is earnings. The three heavy-electric firms (HD Hyundai Electric, Hyosung, LS Electric) posted a combined Q2 2026 operating profit of ₩724.6B — a record quarter, up 53% year over year. Combined backlog approaches ₩35T, four to five years of work. As part 3 showed, a four-year lead time means **volume already sold.** Proven by real numbers, not just hope — that's the bull case's spine, and why it differs from the dot-com bubble or battery stocks.

**The "isn't this a bubble" side.** The problem is valuation. The heavy-electric sector's P/B ran up to 9.2x — far above shipbuilding (4.7x) and defense (5.3x), and even hotter than the multiple shipbuilding got at its 2007 supercycle peak. And there's doubt about the demand itself: reports say that of 12–16GW of planned US data-center capacity, only 5GW has actually broken ground. More than half the plans haven't turned a shovel. If AI capex bends (the debate from the extra installment), inflated demand estimates could collapse, leaving no earnings to support these high multiples.

The two stories sound contradictory, but both hold. **Real demand is genuine — but the stock price (valuation) ran ahead of it.** That's why all three fell over 30% in May–July. Not because earnings soured, but because expectations that ran too far got pulled back — the same pattern as part 8 ([KOSPI concentration](/en/p/kospi-semiconductor/)).

## What the bubble case misses — the triple demand structure

But here's a spot the bubble case often misses. The logic is "if AI data-center investment bends, power stocks are done" — yet power demand doesn't run on AI alone.

![The three demand axes and the bubble-vs-supercycle checklist](demand-and-checklist.png)

Power demand has **three overlapping axes**: ① AI data centers (hottest, but most volatile), ② aging-grid replacement (70% of US transformers are over 25 years old — demand that predates the AI boom), and ③ electrification (EVs and the renewable transition). Strikingly, in Korea's 2038 power-demand outlook, **electrification (11GW) is 2.5x larger than data centers (4.4GW).** AI is just one of three axes; even if the AI theme cools, grid replacement and electrification hold demand up.

This is the decisive difference between chips and power. Chip demand (especially HBM) hinges essentially on AI alone, so an AI downturn is a direct hit. Power's demand base is far broader. "Even if the AI bubble bursts, we still need electricity" is the bull case's core line of defense.

## So what should you watch — four checkpoints

If the answer is "both," the investor's job isn't prophecy but observation. The signals that will decide whether this becomes a supercycle or deflates into a bubble, as a checklist:

- **① Big Tech capex guidance**: the moment upgrades stop is the first signal that the demand source is drying (part 4 / extra-installment logic exactly).
- **② Backlog-to-revenue conversion speed**: how fast ₩35T of backlog turns into actual revenue. Watch the execution risk of "paper orders" becoming cash flow.
- **③ Lead-time direction**: does the 4-year lead time (source of today's premium) hold, or normalize as the world expands capacity (premium gone)? The point flagged in part 3.
- **④ Valuation vs earnings growth**: does earnings growth catch up to a 9x P/B? If not, a correction follows (part 9's price-target logic).

## Recap

- The AI power theme is **a supercycle and a valuation-bubble worry at once.** Real earnings (Q2 three-firm operating profit +53%, ₩35T backlog) are genuine, but a 9.2x P/B is hotter than the shipbuilding supercycle's peak. The May–July 30% correction was that gap snapping.
- The bubble case's weak point is missing the **triple demand structure** — even if AI bends, grid replacement and electrification hold demand up (Korea 2038: electrification 11GW > data centers 4.4GW). That's why power's demand base is broader than chips'.
- Watch a **checklist**, not a prophecy — Big Tech capex, backlog-to-revenue conversion, lead-time direction, valuation vs earnings growth. Same conclusion this series keeps reaching: **buy the theme, but watch where you are in the cycle.**

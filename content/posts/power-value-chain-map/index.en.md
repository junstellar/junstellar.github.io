Over four installments we've run through nuclear (part 2), transformers (part 3), and the bubble debate (part 4). With so many names piling up, it starts to blur. Doosan Enerbility, HD Hyundai Electric, LS Cable… they're all "power stocks," but how do they differ? Today we catch our breath and organize these names onto **a single map** — the same role chip-series part 5 (DRAM/NAND/foundry) played with its industry map.

The map's skeleton is simple: the **three stages** electricity must pass through to reach us — generation, transmission, distribution.

## Electricity's journey — generation → transmission → distribution

Electricity isn't made at a plant and plugged straight into an outlet. It must pass three stages.

![Electricity's journey — from generation to transmission to distribution](electricity-journey.png)

**① Generation — making electricity.** The stage where a plant produces power. Korea's generation mix is nuclear 31.7%, gas 28.1%, coal 28.1%, renewables 10.6% (2025). Part 2's nuclear/SMR, and the gas turbines actually driving earnings, sit here.

**② Transmission — sending it far.** Carrying the power over long distances. To send it far you crank the voltage way up (ultra-high 765kV) — the same principle as a highway letting cars run fast. Part 3's stars, ultra-high-voltage transformers and HVDC undersea cables, are the core of this stage.

**③ Distribution — dividing and delivering.** In front of data centers, factories, and homes, the voltage is brought back down and split up finely — like exiting the highway into neighborhood streets. Switchboards and breakers belong here.

If **any one of the three is blocked, the electricity doesn't arrive.** And as part 1 showed, the AI data-center power crunch created a bottleneck at all three — which is why all three became investment themes. Part 1's "generation, delivery, cooling" three branches are redrawn here in the power system's formal terms (generation, transmission, distribution).

## Where do this series' stocks sit on the map?

Now let's place the companies covered so far onto this map.

![Stock map by stage — generation, transmission, distribution](stock-map.png)

- **Generation (making)**: Doosan Enerbility (nuclear main equipment, gas turbines), KHNP, KEPCO E&C — part 2's protagonists.
- **Transmission (sending)**: HD Hyundai Electric, Hyosung, LS Electric (UHV transformers), LS Cable, Taihan (HVDC undersea cables) — part 3's protagonists.
- **Distribution (dividing)**: LS Electric and others (in-data-center distribution and breakers). And separate from these three stages, **cooling** — dissipating heat — gets its own treatment in part 8.

With the map in your head, the vague phrase "power stocks" stops scattering, because you can see which gate each company stands at. For instance, "if I buy both Doosan Enerbility and HD Hyundai Electric, am I diversified?" The answer is "No — both are AI power plays, but they sit at different gates: generation and transmission." It's the exact same thinking as placing Samsung and hynix on the industry map in chip-series part 5.

## What investors should watch

- **Each gate has a different character**: generation (especially SMR) heavily prices in future earnings (part 2), while transmission (transformers) is already proven in backlog and earnings (part 3). Even within "power stocks," risk and earnings visibility differ.
- **Bottlenecks move**: transmission (4-year transformer lead times) is the hottest bottleneck now, but when it eases, attention shifts to the next one (distribution, cooling, or grid permitting). A map lets you follow that shift.
- **One stock can straddle multiple gates**: the LS group, for example, spans transmission (cable) and distribution (LS Electric). You must check which stage a company actually earns from.

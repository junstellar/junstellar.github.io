## "Shipbuilding and defence are the next leaders" — are they?

One sentence has repeated across Korean research since July: **"the market, which had concentrated excessively in semiconductors, will broaden"** (Yeom Seung-hwan, LS Securities, July 2), naming shipbuilding, defence and power equipment as the next leading sectors.

There's a hidden premise in that sentence: that shipbuilding and defence belong together as **"the not-AI things"** — a refuge for money uncomfortable with AI concentration.

I started from that premise too. Opening it up, the bundle doesn't hold.

![Shipbuilding and defence run on different engines](two-different-engines.png)

## Shipbuilding — a channel into AI actually opened

Start with why shipbuilders are rising. The first reason is simple: **earnings genuinely improved.**

| Q2 2026 | Revenue | Operating profit | |
|---|---|---|---|
| HD Korea Shipbuilding | 8.93tn won (+20.2%) | **1.645tn won** | **+72.5%** |
| Hanwha Ocean | 5.44tn won (+65.2%) | **736.1bn won** | **+98.0%** |
| Samsung Heavy | 3.23tn won (+20.4%) | 325.0bn won | +58.7% |

All three posted record or near-record quarters. This isn't a swing from loss to profit — it's **an existing profit multiplying.** The Clarksons newbuilding price index stood at 185.49 at the end of July, close to the all-time high of about 191.5 set during the 2008 supercycle.

The second reason is America. The **Korea-US Shipbuilding Cooperation Center (KUSPC)** opened in Washington on July 23, following an agreement to direct $150 billion of Korea's $350 billion US investment package into shipbuilding cooperation. On US Navy MRO work, both HD Hyundai Heavy and Hanwha Ocean have already passed last year's totals, and Hanwha has spent $200 million of a planned $5 billion at Philly Shipyard.

**And the third reason is the one that surprised me most.**

![HD Hyundai Heavy's two power-equipment contracts for US data centres](ship-meets-ai.png)

HD Hyundai Heavy Industries is **selling power generation equipment to American data centres.**

- **April 2026** — 684MW of 20MW-class HiMSEN engine generating sets for AEG of the US, **627.1 billion won**
- **August 10, 2026** — 1,000MW of 9.6MW-class HiMSEN sets for Coban Energy Group, **956.0 billion won**, powering a big-tech data centre. It is the largest power-engine contract in the company's history.

That's **1,684MW and roughly 1.58 trillion won in four months.** Samsung Heavy separately obtained approval in principle from US and UK classification societies in April for a 50MW **floating data centre**.

A company that builds ships is selling engines to data centres. In [Power Textbook Episode 1](/en/p/ai-power-hunger/) I wrote that AI's real bottleneck is electricity — that bottleneck has now reached the shipyard.

**But keep the scale honest.** HD Korea Shipbuilding alone booked 8.93 trillion won of revenue in one quarter. 1.58 trillion is still a branch beside the trunk, and Samsung Heavy's floating data centre is a certification, not a commercial order.

**The channel is real, and the channel is still narrow.** The main engine of the shipbuilding rally remains the core business and America. When Choi Kwang-sik of Daol Investment named MASGA and AI data centres as "the two variables that could break through peak-out concerns," he was describing what *could* drive the stocks, not what already is.

## Defence — this one really is unrelated to AI

Defence is a completely different story.

![Q2 operating profit at the defence big four, and the spread inside it](defense-big4.png)

In Q2 2026 the combined operating profit of Korea's four largest defence firms hit **1.752 trillion won**, a record quarter and the fifth straight above 1 trillion. Combined order backlog runs near 100 trillion won.

The driver is geopolitics. Germany's 2026 defence budget rose 25% to €108 billion, and NATO agreed to spend 5% of GDP on security by 2035. Hanwha Aerospace broke ground on a K9 production facility in Romania in February, with local output from 2027, and Hyundai Rotem is negotiating the third tranche of Poland's K2 tank programme (210 units).

**Nowhere in any of that does the word "AI" appear.** There's no channel to data centres the way there is in shipbuilding. Defence isn't *unrelated* to AI so much as **standing on entirely different ground.**

This morning demonstrated it. With the KOSPI down 6%, **Hanwha Aerospace rose 7.42%** — on news it had been selected as sole prototype manufacturer for the US Army's next wheeled self-propelled howitzer (a K9 variant). Six prototypes plus twelve options, worth up to $233 million, with far larger follow-on contracts if it passes trials. **Whether or not the index is collapsing, defence moves on defence news.**

### But look inside the "record"

Per this series' habit, one layer deeper. That 1.752 trillion won breaks down like this.

| | Q2 operating profit | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| Hanwha Aerospace | **1.366tn won** | +58.5% |
| Hyundai Rotem | 232.4bn won | **-9.7%** |
| LIG D&A | 105.7bn won | +29.5% |
| Korea Aerospace (KAI) | 48.4bn won | **-43.1%** |

**Hanwha Aerospace alone produced 77.9% of the combined figure.** And two of the four actually earned less than a year ago.

"Record K-defence earnings" is true, but the place actually earning is narrow. It's the KOSPI concentration from [Index Episode 2](/en/p/index-concentration-trap/) repeating inside a single sector — and a defence ETF holds that spread whole.

## So is now a good time to buy?

This isn't a recommendation, so instead let's just fix the coordinates.

![Shipbuilders' PER and PBR, and where they sit against 52-week highs](valuation-check.png)

Within one sector the multiples diverge sharply. HD Korea Shipbuilding trades at 13.28x earnings and 2.01x book; Samsung Heavy at 38.89x and 4.96x. **Hard to bundle those under one word.**

All four also sit **26–43% below their 52-week highs.** Defence is similar: Hanwha Aerospace was 40.3% below its high for the year as of early August.

That reads two ways — the same problem as [Index Episode 6](/en/p/reading-index-ai-era/). Earnings improved while prices fell, so either it got cheap, or the market doesn't believe the earnings ahead. Brokerages split. iM Securities cut Hanwha Aerospace's target from 1.83m to 1.49m won while calling the valuation **"as attractive relative to fundamentals as it has ever been"** (analyst Byun Yong-jin); KB Securities cut from 1.75m to 1.40m, down 20%. On the shipbuilding side, Kiwoom, Shinhan and Yuanta all trimmed targets too.

**Improving earnings, falling target prices.** The multiple is being cut rather than the earnings — precisely the grammar we saw in semiconductors in Index Episode 6.

## Summary

- **The "shipbuilding and defence" bundle doesn't hold.** They're always named together as rotation beneficiaries, but they run on different engines.
- **Shipbuilding has genuinely opened a channel into AI.** HD Hyundai Heavy won 1.58 trillion won of data-centre power equipment orders in four months — though that's still small beside quarterly revenue in the 8-trillion range.
- **The body of the shipbuilding rally is the core business and America.** Q2 operating profit up 58–98%, newbuild prices near the 2008 peak, and new markets in MASGA, navy MRO and Philly Shipyard.
- **Defence really is unrelated to AI.** European rearmament and Middle East demand are the whole story — proven this morning, when Hanwha Aerospace rose 7.42% into a 6% market drop.
- **Look inside the "record."** Hanwha Aerospace produced 77.9% of the big four's combined operating profit; two of the four earned less.

So "money uncomfortable with AI concentration is going into shipbuilding and defence" is only half right. **One is extending toward AI; the other was standing on different ground all along.** If they weren't bought for the same reason, they won't share a fate when semiconductors run again.

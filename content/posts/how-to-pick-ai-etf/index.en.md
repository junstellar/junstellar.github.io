Through [Episode 4](/en/p/leverage-etf-trap/) this series has been about what *not* to do. Now we turn it around: **how do you actually choose?**

Up front: I'm not going to tell you to buy a particular ETF. Instead I'll show you **how to open one up.** Holdings shift constantly, so the habit of checking will serve you far longer than any number I write today.

## First, don't trust the word "AI"

Korea has plenty of ETFs with "AI" in the name. But the word points to something different in each one.

![Three Korean ETFs where the AI in the name means something different](what-ai-means.png)

- **AI as the *selection method*** — TIGER AI Korea Growth Active means an AI algorithm picks the holdings. What's actually inside is large-cap growth names.
- **AI as the *end customer*** — KODEX AI Semiconductor Core Equipment holds companies that build **equipment** for making AI chips. Not AI software firms.
- **AI as the *cause of demand*** — KODEX AI Power Core Equipment is themed on the electricity demand AI data centers created; the actual holdings are power equipment makers. (The very companies covered in the [AI Power textbook](/en/p/ai-power-hunger/).)

All three are "AI ETFs" and all three hold entirely different things. **The name is marketing; the holdings are the product.**

## Even "semiconductor ETFs" are built differently

Go one level deeper and the concentration varies enormously.

![Fees and concentration compared across major Korean semiconductor ETFs](semi-etf-compare.png)

- **KODEX Semiconductor (091160)** — 0.5238% annual fee. Spread across roughly 30 names, but Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix together are around half.
- **TIGER Fn Semiconductor TOP10 (396500)** — 0.45% annual fee. Ten holdings, and the FnGuide index it tracks applies a **25% cap to each of the top two names.**
- **ACE Global Semiconductor TOP4 Plus (446770)** — 0.45% annual fee. SK Hynix, ASML, Nvidia and TSMC make up **roughly 80%** — one champion each from memory, equipment, GPUs and foundry.

Which brings [Episode 2](/en/p/index-concentration-trap/) to mind. The KOSPI 200 **has no weight cap.** But **some individual ETFs do.** You can't change the index, but if concentration bothers you, choosing a capped product is an available option.

**A cap isn't a perfect shield, though.** It's enforced at quarterly rebalancing, so a sharp move in between can push a holding temporarily above the limit.

And please remember: **the numbers above are from when I checked.** Weights move with prices and rebalancing. Look them up before you buy.

## Does buying a semiconductor ETF separately even make sense?

Episodes 1 and 2 established that the KOSPI itself is already more than half AI semiconductors. So what does adding a semiconductor ETF accomplish?

Honestly: **it overlaps.** If you hold a KOSPI 200 ETF and add a Korean semiconductor ETF, you may simply be increasing the same two positions.

It makes sense in cases like these:
- **You want Korean semiconductor suppliers too** — names like Hanmi Semiconductor and Leeno Industrial are small in the KOSPI 200 but larger in a semiconductor ETF.
- **You want global exposure** — ASML, Nvidia and TSMC aren't in the KOSPI 200. Global semiconductor ETFs hold them.
- **You want a capped product** to soften the concentration, as above.

But if the reasoning is "AI looks promising, I'll grab a semiconductor ETF too," the first step is checking how much it duplicates what you already own.

## Tax — this can matter more than returns

The most commonly overlooked part. **The same index is taxed completely differently depending on where you buy it** (rules below are for Korean residents).

![Tax comparison across domestic equity, domestically listed foreign, and directly held overseas ETFs](etf-tax.png)

| | Domestic equity ETF | Domestically listed foreign ETF | Overseas-listed ETF, held directly |
|---|---|---|---|
| Example | KODEX 200 | TIGER US Nasdaq 100 | QQQ, SOXX |
| Capital gains | **Exempt** | 15.4% | 22%, with 2.5m won annual deduction |
| Distributions | 15.4% | 15.4% | 15.4% |
| Global income aggregation | Distributions only | **Capital gains included too** | Distributions only (gains taxed separately) |
| Offsetting gains/losses | N/A | **Not allowed** | **Allowed** within the same year |

Several practical implications.

**① For small amounts, direct overseas holding can win.** The 2.5 million won annual deduction means gains below that are untaxed.

**② At larger amounts the math flips.** Past the deduction it's 22%, versus 15.4% for a domestically listed foreign ETF. On rate alone the latter is lower.

**③ But high financial income flips it back.** Domestically listed foreign ETFs fold capital gains into global income taxation. Above 20 million won of annual financial income, progressive rates can reach 49.5%. Overseas capital gains are taxed separately and stay out of that pool.

**④ Loss offsetting differs.** With direct overseas holdings, a loss on A can offset a gain on B in the same year. Domestically listed foreign ETFs are taxed position by position with no offsetting.

And the one that stings most: **distributions are taxed even when you're losing money.** Trading losses don't offset distribution income. You can be down on the position and still owe tax.

## Changing the account changes the tax

The same product in a different account produces a different after-tax result.

- **Pension savings / IRP**: taxes are deferred inside the account, and you pay only 3.3–5.5% when drawing it as a pension. But **leveraged and inverse ETFs cannot be bought at all**, and an IRP caps risk assets at 70% (a pension savings fund allows 100%).
- **ISA**: gains and losses inside the account are netted before taxation, which softens the offsetting problem described above.

For context, the **tax reform proposal announced on August 3, 2026** includes an ISA overhaul: a new **"productive finance ISA"** for domestic stocks and equity funds that would exempt interest and dividend income entirely, with a 10% income deduction on contributions for young subscribers. The existing general ISA would keep its 20 million won annual limit but be capped at five years total, with the carry-forward of unused limits abolished.

Note that this is **still a proposal awaiting passage**, with the effective date set for new subscriptions from January 2027. Treat it as background until confirmed.

## Five minutes before you buy — five things

![Five things to check before buying an ETF](etf-checklist.png)

**① The top ten holdings.** Not the name — what's actually inside, and **how much it duplicates what you already hold.**

**② Headline fee versus real cost.** Advertised fees center on the management fee. What you actually pay includes other expenses and trading costs, and the gap tends to widen for products holding overseas assets.

**③ Net assets and turnover.** If an ETF past its first year falls below **5 billion won** in net assets and doesn't recover by the next half-year end, it becomes a delisting candidate. Thin trading also means worse fills when you sell. (Note that a delisted ETF settles at net asset value — it doesn't go to zero like a failed stock.)

**④ Premium/discount to NAV.** Korea's exchange requires disclosure when it exceeds 1% for domestic products and 2% for overseas ones. It's distinct from tracking error: **premium/discount asks "is today's price fair," tracking error asks "does this fund follow its index well."**

**⑤ Tax category and account.** Domestic or foreign for tax purposes, and whether it's eligible for a pension account or ISA.

## One lesson from last month

In July 2026, **891 Korean-listed ETFs — 78.3% of them — fell.** Semiconductor theme ETFs were hit hardest, with several down 36–38%, and the bottom of the table was entirely single-stock leverage (worst: −68.7%).

Meanwhile, in the US the same month: the semiconductor ETF **SOXX fell 22.1%, its worst month since 2002** — and took in **$6.9 billion of net inflows in July**, a record.

In the same drawdown, one group was liquidated and the other accumulated. The difference wasn't stock selection. It was **how they were holding it.**

## Summary

- **Don't trust the label.** "AI" may refer to the selection algorithm, the end customer, or the source of demand. The holdings are the product.
- **Semiconductor ETFs differ structurally.** Some are half Samsung and Hynix; one caps its top two at 25%; another puts 80% in four names. **The KOSPI 200 has no weight cap — but some ETFs do.**
- **Tax matters as much as return.** Domestic equity ETFs are exempt on gains; domestically listed foreign ETFs pay 15.4% and get folded into global income; direct overseas holdings pay 22% with a deduction and allow loss offsetting. Which wins flips with size and your other income.
